# BOOK SYNOPSIS

## Logline
A rope access pipefitter with no programming background promises his sister—who has an incurable neurological disease—that he'll build "a legion of minds" smart enough to help her, then teaches himself to code, invents new mathematics, and creates a sovereign AI ecosystem while working full-time and pursuing two degrees.

## Extended Synopsis

THE SISTER PROTOCOL opens with a drunken promise and a cutting challenge: "You can't even program. You don't even know how to use the command line." The speaker is the author's sister, born with one arm and diagnosed with an incurable neurological condition. The recipient is Domenic Garza, a pipefitter who had never touched a terminal in his life.

That night, Dom searched "hardest command line to learn" and found vim—a notoriously difficult text editor. He installed it, tried to type, discovered he was trapped in a mode he didn't understand, and spent twenty minutes trying to exit. That humiliation became fuel.

**Part I: Origins** traces Dom's journey from CLI ignorance to obsessive mastery. He makes five vows: no graphical interfaces, version everything, build for succession, hardcode the mission into legal DNA, and invert any approach that has already failed. These vows transform a personal promise into permanent infrastructure.

**Part II: The Breakthrough Loop** explores the notebooks that preceded the math—geometric sketches, frequency diagrams, DNA parallels—and the emergence of "Quadrilateral Collapse Learning," Dom's name for his own cognitive architecture: symbolic, spatial, narrative, and kinesthetic channels firing simultaneously.

**Part III: The Math** introduces TRIG6, a novel mathematical framework that treats AI agent states as angles on a manifold, using trigonometric functions to derive metrics like "resonance" (health), "drift" (deviation), and "danger zones" (system instability). It also maps 100 AI engineering bottlenecks across 10 pillars, creating what Dom calls his "Newton's Principia for AI."

**Part IV: The Machines** details the technical stack: FlameLang (a 5-layer compiler transforming English → Hebrew roots → Unicode numerics → wave frequencies → DNA codons → LLVM binaries), SAGCO-OS (a sovereign operating system versioned as DNA strands), and the Legion of Minds (a multi-AI consensus system using Claude, GPT, Grok, and local models).

**Part V: The Mission** returns to the sister. Dom maps 36 incurable neurological diseases (NEURO-36) into TRIG6 parameters, treating EEG signatures as wave inputs to his compiler. He creates ValorYield Engine PBC with a 7% irrevocable charitable allocation—hardcoded into the operating agreement and impossible to remove.

**Part VI: The Future** asks what happens when the system starts working. Who owns discoveries made by a legion of minds? How does a pipefitter from Texas navigate patent law, academic publishing, and medical research ethics? And what does "help" even mean when the problem is this big?

The book ends with a letter to Dom's sister—the original audience, the reason for everything, and the only KPI that matters: Did it help?
