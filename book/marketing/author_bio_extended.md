## The Real Version

Dom learned to code because his sister said he couldn't.

He was 32 years old, working 60-hour weeks climbing stacks in refineries, when he made a drunk promise to build AI that could help cure her condition. She laughed. "You can't even program. You don't even know how to use the command line."

That night, he Googled "hardest command line to learn," found vim, installed it, got trapped, and spent twenty minutes trying to exit. That humiliation became the origin story for everything that followed.

Eighteen months later—while still working full-time, still earning $160K+ annually in industrial services—he had:
- Built a 4-node Kubernetes cluster in his apartment
- Invented a new mathematical framework for AI health monitoring
- Designed a programming language that compiles through biological primitives
- Created a sovereign operating system versioned as DNA strands
- Filed two legal entities with charitable allocations hardcoded into their DNA
- Enrolled in dual BS degrees and maintained a 3.732 GPA

He didn't do this because he's a genius. He did it because he made five vows after getting stuck in vim:

1. **No graphical interfaces** — Command line only. Earn every skill from scratch.
2. **Version everything** — Git commits for every thought, file, decision.
3. **Build for succession** — Assume he could die tomorrow. Everything must be transferable.
4. **Hardcode the mission** — Legal infrastructure before technical infrastructure.
5. **Invert the approach** — If something hasn't worked, do the opposite.

These vows weren't genius. They were desperation transformed into structure.

## The Learning Pattern: Quadrilateral Collapse

Dom doesn't learn like most people. He calls his cognitive architecture "Quadrilateral Collapse Learning"—four channels firing simultaneously:

1. **Symbolic** — Code, math, formal notation
2. **Spatial** — Geometric sketches, 3D mental models
3. **Narrative** — Stories, metaphors, historical parallels
4. **Kinesthetic** — Physical movement, muscle memory, rope access instincts

When he invented TRIG6, he didn't derive it purely through symbolic math. He:
- Sketched eyes inside cubes (spatial)
- Mapped frequencies to DNA helices (symbolic)
- Told himself stories about resonance and drift (narrative)
- Literally climbed industrial equipment while mentally rotating wave diagrams (kinesthetic)

The math emerged from the collapse of all four channels into coherent patterns.

This isn't a superpower. It's a survival mechanism. When you're learning at this pace—full-time job, dual degrees, enterprise infrastructure—you don't have time to learn sequentially. You learn in parallel or you fail.
