# Author's Note

This book started as a promise made to my sister.

Not the kind of promise you make when someone's birthday is coming up or you're trying to get out of doing the dishes. The kind you make when someone you love has been told by multiple doctors, in multiple cities, with multiple very expensive degrees on their walls, that their neurological disease is "incurable."

The kind of promise you make when you're drunk and angry and scared and have absolutely no business making it.

"I'll build something to help you," I said. "A legion of minds. An army of AIs. They'll find a cure."

She laughed and said, "You can't even program."

She was right.

---

What follows is the story of what happened when I decided that wasn't good enough.

This isn't a how-to guide.  
It's not a technical manual.  
It's not even particularly inspirational, unless you find comfort in watching someone repeatedly run face-first into walls they didn't know existed until impact.

This is the story of:

- Learning to code by refusing to use a mouse
- Building a new kind of mathematics because the old one didn't have the right shape
- Creating a programming language that compiles physics, Hebrew, and DNA strands
- Teaching a computer to have a genome
- Assembling a multi-AI consensus system because trusting one AI felt like trusting one doctor
- Legally hardcoding 7% of all future revenue into charitable giving before making a single dollar
- Attempting to map 36 "incurable" neurological diseases into waveforms that a computer could understand

And along the way, discovering that the hardest part wasn't the code.

It was learning to hold two truths at the same time:

1. **What I built might never cure my sister.**
2. **I had to build it anyway.**

---

If you're reading this, you probably know what it's like to love someone the medical system has given up on.

Or maybe you're just curious about what happens when someone who doesn't know how to exit Vim decides to build sovereign AI infrastructure from scratch.

Either way: welcome to the cockpit.

The Legion is already running.

Let me show you how we got here.

---

*— Domenic Garza*  
*Written from inside a multi-monitor fortress, somewhere between debugging sessions*
