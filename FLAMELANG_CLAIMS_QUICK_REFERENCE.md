## Do's and Don'ts

### ✅ DO SAY:

**For Claims 5 & 6 (True Firsts):**
- "First multi-domain compilation pipeline integrating linguistics, physics, biology, and silicon"
- "First programming language specification ratified by AI systems"
- "Novel cross-domain semantic preservation methodology"
- "Unprecedented AI governance model for technical standards"

**For Claims 2, 3, 7 (Novel Variants):**
- "Novel ISA-level unit enforcement approach" (if proven)
- "First type system for entangled quantum states as primitives"
- "Novel synthesis of autonomic computing with AI sovereignty"

**For Claims 1 & 4 (Must Acknowledge Prior Art):**
- "Building on Deoxyribose's codon compilation, FlameLang adds LLVM native compilation..."
- "Inspired by APL's glyph-based approach, FlameLang introduces Hebrew linguistic semantics..."
- "Extends prior art with [specific improvement]"

### ❌ DON'T SAY:

**Never claim without acknowledgment:**
- ~~"First biological compilation layer"~~ (Deoxyribose 2019)
- ~~"First glyph-based programming language"~~ (APL 1962)
- ~~"First compile-time physics type system"~~ (F# 2005)
- ~~"First native quantum primitives"~~ (Q# 2017)
- ~~"First organism-metaphor compute system"~~ (IBM 2001)

**Avoid vague claims:**
- ~~"World's first"~~ without specifics
- ~~"Revolutionary"~~ without differentiation
- ~~"Unprecedented"~~ when prior art exists

## Common Questions & Answers

**Q: Can we claim "world first" for FlameLang overall?**
A: No. Use specific claims only: "First multi-domain scientific compilation pipeline" and "First AI-ratified language specification."

**Q: Do we have to cite prior art in marketing materials?**
A: Legal requirement: Yes for patents. Ethical requirement: Yes for academic integrity. Recommended: Yes for credibility.

**Q: What if we modify the prior art significantly?**
A: Still must cite it. Focus claims on specific improvements and differentiators.

**Q: Can we claim "inspired by" instead of citing?**
A: For academic papers: Must cite formally. For marketing: "Inspired by" is acceptable but weaker than clear differentiation.

**Q: What about claims we haven't implemented yet?**
A: Cannot file patent claims without reduction to practice (working implementation or detailed specification).
