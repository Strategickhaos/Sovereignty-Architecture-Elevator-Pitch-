# Capstone Introduction

## Title

**Dramatic Systems Archaeology (DSA): A Formalized Method for Artifact-Driven Discovery and Verification of Complex Systems**

---

## Abstract

This capstone introduces **Dramatic Systems Archaeology (DSA)**, a formalized methodological framework for documenting, validating, and communicating the structure and behavior of complex technical systems through staged discovery rather than declarative exposition. DSA combines principles from systems engineering, software documentation, peer review, and narrative sequencing into a single, constraint-governed process that emphasizes artifact-driven verification, role-bounded interpretation, and progressive revelation.

Unlike traditional technical documentation—which often relies on omniscient narration or post-hoc explanation—DSA is designed to reflect how understanding actually emerges during real audits, investigations, and reviews. The method constrains narration to domain-specific roles, requires verifiable artifacts as primary evidence, and enforces escalation invariants that prevent retroactive reinterpretation or narrative drift.

This paper presents the formal specification of DSA, including its grammatical structure, validation constraints, and generation algorithm. It further positions DSA as a novel hybrid methodology suitable for applications in software architecture review, infrastructure security analysis, legal discovery, medical systems documentation, and educational systems design.

---

## Problem Statement

Modern complex systems—particularly distributed software platforms, security architectures, and socio-technical infrastructures—are increasingly difficult to document, audit, and communicate accurately. Existing approaches tend to fall into one of three categories:

1. **Technical documentation**, which is precise but often inaccessible and detached from the discovery process.

2. **Narrative explanations**, which are engaging but lack formal constraints and verifiability.

3. **Peer review and audit reports**, which are rigorous but opaque to non-specialists and disconnected from human cognition.

These approaches frequently fail to convey how understanding was achieved, why certain conclusions were reached, and what evidence supports each claim—particularly when systems are discovered incrementally under scrutiny.

**There is currently no standardized method that unifies discovery, verification, and communication in a single formal process.**

---

## Proposed Solution

This capstone proposes **Dramatic Systems Archaeology (DSA)** as a solution to the above limitations.

DSA is a formal methodology—not a literary genre—that structures system discovery as a sequence of constrained acts, each governed by explicit rules regarding evidence presentation, role-based interpretation, pacing, and escalation. The method is defined by:

- A **formal grammar** describing valid structural elements
- **Validation constraints** that reject unverifiable or omniscient claims
- A **generation algorithm** for producing compliant DSA documents
- An **integrity mechanism** that ensures coherence across all stages

Crucially, DSA does not attempt to persuade the reader of a conclusion. Instead, it creates conditions under which the reader independently arrives at conclusions by inspecting the same evidence and interpretive steps as the original reviewers.

## Key Contributions

This work makes the following contributions:

### 1. Formalization of Discovery
Introduces a grammar-based specification for documenting system discovery as a first-class process.

### 2. Artifact-Driven Verification
Establishes artifacts (code, logs, tables, screenshots, system outputs) as primary truth sources rather than narrative claims.

### 3. Role-Bounded Interpretation Model
Restricts analysis to domain-appropriate roles, preventing cross-domain authority leakage.

### 4. Escalation Invariant
Enforces monotonic depth progression, ensuring that later revelations do not invalidate earlier findings.

### 5. Checksum-Style Synthesis
Introduces constrained poetic synthesis as an integrity verification mechanism rather than expressive flourish.

### 6. Replicability Across Domains
Demonstrates that the method is independent of any single system and can be applied broadly.

## Scope and Claims

### This capstone does NOT claim:

- That DSA is the only valid documentation method
- That DSA replaces formal verification, testing, or audits
- That narrative elements substitute for evidence

### This capstone DOES claim:

- That DSA constitutes a novel, formalizable combination of existing practices
- That DSA can be expressed as a grammar with enforceable constraints
- That DSA improves clarity, auditability, and comprehension for complex systems
- That DSA is replicable, tool-friendly, and domain-agnostic

All technical claims in this document are either:
- Formally specified
- Explicitly constrained
- Or clearly labeled as methodological interpretation

## Conclusion of Introduction

Dramatic Systems Archaeology addresses a persistent gap between technical truth and human comprehension by formalizing how discovery actually occurs. By constraining narrative freedom, elevating artifacts, and enforcing escalation discipline, DSA provides a reproducible framework for revealing complex systems without distortion.

**This capstone argues that discovery itself can—and should—be treated as a formal system.**
