## The Traditional Approach

When confronted with a challenge—whether curing a disease, building software, or running an organization—the traditional instinct is:

1. **Focus on success** - What's the ideal outcome?
2. **Ignore failure** - Don't dwell on what could go wrong
3. **Move fast** - Velocity matters more than resilience
4. **Optimize for metrics** - Dashboard green? Ship it.

**Result:** Systems that look good on paper but collapse under stress. Diseases that are "managed" but never cured. Organizations that drift from their mission despite good intentions.

---

## The Inverse Principle

The Sister Protocol inverts this:

1. **Map every failure first** - What are the 36 ways this could break?
2. **Study the wound** - What does the failure teach us?
3. **Wait for correctness** - Don't rush; the chain is the quality gate
4. **Measure what matters** - "Did it help?" overrides all other KPIs

**Result:** Antifragile systems that **improve from failures**. Wounds that become weapons. Organizations that cannot drift from their mission because the mission is encoded in math and law.

I didn't start with TRIG6. I started with a family member's seizure—uncontrolled, unpredictable, life-altering. The neurologist said: *"We don't really understand why anti-seizure medications work. We just try different ones until something helps."*

**The question became:** What if diseases aren't mysteries—they're just equations we haven't solved yet?

And if they're equations, then **failures are just unsolved variables**.
