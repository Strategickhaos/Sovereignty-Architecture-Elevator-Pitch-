## Category 1 — Papyrus-Style Paper (12 Blueprints)

Modern adaptations of Egyptian papyrus: pith/inner fibers cross-layered, hammered/pressed, dried under weight, then polished.

### 1. Basic Reed Papyrus

Harvest reeds, strip inner pith, soak briefly, layer strips crosswise on a flat surface, hammer/press, then dry under heavy weight for ~3 days.

### 2. Wetland Grass Variant

Use local marsh/wetland grass, soak 5–7 days, flatten and weave strips, roll with a bottle or rolling pin, sun-dry 1–2 days.

### 3. Bamboo Adaptation

Split thin bamboo slivers, boil in mildly alkaline water (e.g., lime), rinse, lay orthogonally, press 24 hours, then polish with a smooth stone.

### 4. Banana Stem Sheets

Peel banana stems, chop inner fibers, blend with water into pulp, pour onto a mesh frame, drain, dry on cloth, and iron smooth at low heat.

### 5. Cotton Rag Paper

Shred old cotton cloth, soak overnight, blend to a fine pulp, scoop onto a deckle mold, press, air-dry, then burnish (or run through a "calender"—rolling pressure) for smoothness.

### 6. Hemp Fiber Sheets

Ret old hemp stalks (soak and soften), boil, beat to fibrils, spread in thin layers, hammer gently, dry in shade to avoid brittleness.

### 7. Mulberry Bark Paper

Strip inner mulberry bark, boil with wood ash, rinse thoroughly, beat into pulp, form sheets on a screen, dry on flat boards.

### 8. Rice Straw Pulp

Chop rice straw, ferment for ~3 days, boil, blend, pour into a mold, press between absorbent sheets, air-dry.

### 9. Corn Husk Composite

Soak corn husks, blend with water, optionally mix with small amount of starch, form sheets, press, then oven-dry at low temperature.

### 10. Sugarcane Bagasse Paper

Use processed sugarcane residue, boil to remove sugars, pulp, layer crosswise, press, and sun-dry.

### 11. Simple Wood Pulp

Soak small wood chips, digest in soda solution (sodium hydroxide–based), rinse, form into sheets, dry and roll.

### 12. Recycled Paper Rebirth

Shred waste paper, soak, de-ink as possible, blend to pulp, add starch binder, form sheets in a mold, press, and dry.

## Category 2 — Coptic / Link-Stitch Binding (12 Blueprints)

Codex evolution from scrolls: folded signatures sewn through the fold with visible chain-link stitches; early precedents seen in Coptic/Nag Hammadi bindings.

### 13. Basic Coptic Stitch

Fold signatures (4–8 sheets each), clamp, punch sewing stations with an awl, use waxed linen thread to sew chain-stitches across the spine, then attach front and back boards.

### 14. Single-Needle Link Stitch

Use a single curved needle, link each signature to the previous one; optionally glue the spine lightly and wrap in a flexible papyrus cover.

### 15. Double-Thread Coptic

Work with two threads in mirror paths for extra strength; finish with kettle stitches at the ends and attach wooden boards wrapped in leather.

### 16. Nag Hammadi-Style Replica

Papyrus or handmade sheets as signatures, simple sewing with linen thread through a small number of stations, then encase in a tanned leather cover with a wrap-around flap and thong tie.

### 17. Modern Recycled Coptic

Recycled paper signatures, printed manuscript pages, punched using a jig, sewn with strong synthetic thread; covers made from bookboard or cardboard wrapped with cloth.

### 18. Exposed Spine Coptic

No spine covering; stitches fully visible as decorative element, with possible colored endbands; leather or board covers attached only at sewing stations.

### 19. Multi-Section Link Binding

Eight or more signatures, evenly spaced stations, figure-8 link stitch, carefully creased folds using a bone folder for clean openings.

### 20. Parchment-Style Adaptation

Use vellum or thick technical paper, prick sewing holes in advance, sew onto thin cords or tapes, attach stout wooden boards.

### 21. Scroll-to-Codex Hybrid

Core remains rolled (scroll-like), but edges of layers are intermittently stitched and bound into a partial codex with leather straps—symbolic transition from scroll to code(x).

### 22. Reinforced Spine Coptic

Sew signatures onto cloth tapes; after sewing, glue a strip of mull (cheesecloth) to the spine for strength before adding covers.

### 23. Decorative Pattern Coptic

Use contrasting colored threads, incorporate simple geometric or wave patterns across the exposed spine; add gold leaf or pyrography to wooden boards, with papyrus inlays.

### 24. Miniature Sister Codex

Small-format version: tiny signatures, fine needle, silk thread, and miniature wood or walnut shell covers—"amulet" sized Sister Protocol.

## Category 3 — Glue, Stitching, and Leather (12 Blueprints)

Materials echo ancient practice: plant starch glues, linen/hemp/silk threads, and leathers tanned with vegetable tannins or alum.

### 25. Wheat Starch Paste Glue

Mix wheat flour and water (~1:5), cook until translucent, let cool; yields a reversible starch paste (polymeric (C₆H₁₀O₅)ₙ) suitable for paper.

### 26. Reed-Sap Gum

Crush fresh reeds, extract sap, gently boil with a bit of honey or sugar; produces a natural gum resin adhesive.

### 27. Animal Gelatin Glue

Boil clean bones and skins, skim impurities, reduce to jelly; reheat small amounts with water for a traditional collagen-based glue.

### 28. Acacia / Gum Arabic Tannin Glue

Grind acacia bark or use gum arabic, dissolve in water, optionally ferment briefly; tannin-rich binder that adheres well to porous fibers.

### 29. Linen/Flax Thread

Process flax fibers, spin into threads, lightly wax with beeswax; strong cellulose-based stitching material.

### 30. Hemp Thread Variant

Ret and boil hemp fibers, spin and ply them, finish with oil or wax; yields tough, abrasion-resistant thread.

### 31. Silk Book Thread

Reel silk filaments from cocoons, twist into fine thread, optionally dye; strong yet flexible, ideal for miniature or decorative codices.

### 32. Alum-Tanned Goat Leather

Soak and clean goat hide, tan with alum (KAl(SO₄)₂·12H₂O) and salt, stretch and dry; produces pale, flexible cover leather.

### 33. Vegetable-Tanned Leather

Soak hides in oak or other bark tannins for weeks; tannins cross-link collagen to produce durable, firm cover leather.

### 34. Brain-Tanned and Smoked Leather

Rub emulsified animal brains into the hide, work and stretch repeatedly, then smoke over a low fire; yields very soft, flexible leather.

### 35. Chrome-Tanned Variant

Treat hides with chromium sulfate solutions (Cr₂(SO₄)₃) for faster, flexible leather; more modern, less historically Egyptian, but accessible.

### 36. Synthetic "Leather" Alternative

Use layers of cloth impregnated with PVA glue; press and dry to form a pseudo-leather sheet—good for vegan or low-resource builds.

### Workflow

#### 1. Print core chapters

Print core chapters (Chapter 1, Chapter 14, proposal, NEURO-36 overview).

#### 2. Recycled paper overlay

Make a small batch of handmade sheets from shredded scrap, use them as:

- title page
- section dividers
- final "Did It Help?" page in your own handwriting.

#### 3. Signatures

- Fold printed pages into signatures (e.g., 4 sheets → 16 pages per sig).
- Interleave the handmade sheets as opening/end leaves for each part.

#### 4. Punch + Sew

- Mark 4–6 sewing stations along the fold.
- Use an awl, then sew Coptic style with linen thread, leaving spine exposed.

#### 5. Covers

- Cut board to size, wrap with veg-tan leather, optionally emboss the DNA strand:
  ```
  SAGCO-ATG-FLM2-…-SISTER1-BOOK1
  ```

#### 6. Glue

- Use wheat paste for cover attachment and endpapers.
- Let dry under weight 24–48 hours.
