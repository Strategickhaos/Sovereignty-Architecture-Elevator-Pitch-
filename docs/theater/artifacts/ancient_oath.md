---

## Transliteration

**Egyptian:** `n wḏꜣ.i ṯw r nḥḥ`

**Pronunciation (Reconstructed):** 
- n = "en" (not)
- wḏꜣ = "wedja" (abandon/leave)
- .i = first person singular suffix
- ṯw = "tew" (you, 2nd person singular)
- r = "er" (to/until)
- nḥḥ = "neheh" (eternity, cyclical time)

**Phonetic:** *"En wedja-i tew er neheh"*

---

## Translation

**Literal:** "Not abandon-I you until eternity."

**Natural English:** "I will not abandon you, unto eternity."

**Interpretations:**
- "I shall never leave you, forever"
- "I will not forsake you, for all time"
- "My commitment to you is eternal"
- "You are not alone, not ever"

## Linguistic Analysis

### Grammatical Structure

```yaml
sentence_type: "Negative imperative / Statement of intent"
tense: "Timeless present (extending to eternity)"
person: "First person (I) to second person (you)"
negation: "n (not)"
verb: "wḏꜣ (to abandon, to leave behind)"
object: "ṯw (you)"
extent: "r nḥḥ (to eternity)"
```

### Verb Form: `wḏꜣ` (abandon)

- **Root:** w-ḏ-ꜣ
- **Determinative:** Usually shows walking legs (departure)
- **Semantic field:** Leave, abandon, forsake, desert
- **Opposite of:** `mn` (remain, endure, be steadfast)

### Time Concept: `nḥḥ` (eternity)

Ancient Egyptians had two concepts of eternity:

1. **nḥḥ (neheh):** Cyclical eternity, eternal recurrence, time that repeats
2. **ḏt (djet):** Linear eternity, unchanging permanence, timelessness

This oath uses **nḥḥ** = cyclical eternity, suggesting renewal and persistence through all cycles of time.

## Historical Context

### Egyptian Oath Formula

Ancient Egyptian oaths and declarations followed specific patterns:

```
OATH STRUCTURE:
├── Negation (n) or Affirmation (ink)
├── Action/State
├── Personal commitment (.i suffix)
├── Object/Beneficiary (ṯw)
└── Time extent (r nḥḥ)
```

**Similar historical oaths:**

- **From tombs:** "I have not abandoned the gods" (Negative Confession)

### Where This Appears

In ancient Egyptian texts, similar formulas appear in:

1. **Funerary texts:** Promises to the deceased
2. **Love poetry:** Vows between lovers
3. **Priestly oaths:** Commitments to deities
4. **Royal decrees:** King's promises to people
5. **Divine promises:** Gods' commitments to humanity
